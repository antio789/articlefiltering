TITLE: Effect of ultrasonic assisted KOH pretreatment on physiochemical characteristic and anaerobic digestion performance of wheat straw


1 Introduction

Keeping in view the role of fossil fuels on environmental pollution, bioenergy from agricultural waste has been recognized as a potentially important alternative energy resource. Lignocellulosic based biomass such as wheat straw (WS), is a naturally available and an abundant resource for biomethane production, using anaerobic digestion process. However, the complex intermolecular attachments between lignin and other organic portions, such as cellulose and hemicellulose, show strong resistance to effective biodegradation of WS [1].

A large number of pretreatments, such as physical, chemical, biological and so on, are reported to reduce the effects of structural hindrance from lignin by increasing the contact area of organic matter present in the substrate for anaerobic microbes. Mechanical pretreatment enhances the specific surface area by reducing the degree of crystallinity [2]. The complex portion of structurally rigid part of the lignocellulosic biomass (lignin) can be successfully disrupted by wet explosion pretreatment [3]. Hydrothermal pretreatments of WS disrupt the xylan in hemicellulose due to exposure to high temperature [4]. Similarly, chemical pretreatments have physiochemical effects on intermolecular linkages between lignocellulose structures. Compared with acidic pretreatments, alkaline pretreatments such as calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)_2), sodium hydroxide (NaOH), and potassium hydroxide (KOH), are more suitable due to less inhibitory products and stable digester performances [5]. The alkali pretreatment severely affects the ester bonding in between the lignin and hemicellulose components, by reducing the possible eradication of polymer linkages in the hemicellulose content [6]. Therefore, the effective disruption of ester bonds is possible without further fragmentations in the hemicellulose polymers through alkali pretreatments [6,7]. Moreover, using KOH is beneficial for enriched digestate values in agricultural applications, as bio-fertilizer [8]. However, the effectiveness of each pretreatment technique applied, mainly depends upon the type of lignocellulosic biomass and pretreatment conditions.

Low concentration alkali pretreatment for lignocellulosic based biomass may not efficiently influence on the structural hindrance of lignin, which is main recalcitrant in the digester performance. Consequently, some amount of biodegradable substrate is still present in digester effluents. In contrast, high alkali concentrations release some phenolic and heterocyclic compounds of furfural resulting in inhibition and slower performance by anaerobic microbes [9,10]. Shetty et al. [11] explained the effect of high NaOH concentration on methane yield due to increasing Na^+ ion resulting in dehydration of anaerobic microbes. As a result, the anaerobic digestion process shows limited biogasification with lesser methane production. Therefore, effective disruption of lignocellulosic content can be possible by combining two pretreatments using lower alkali concentrations to increase the biodegradation during digestion process [12]. Yu et al. [13] compared the studies of different pretreatment methods for crop straw and suggested that combined pretreatment has great potential to enhance disruption and yield more biogas. Alkaline pretreatments coupled with thermal applications are helpful in increasing biogas productions along with high methane content. Sambusiti et al. [14] reported 32% and 43% increase in methane productions by applying thermo-alkaline pretreatment on sorghum and WS respectively. Yuan et al. [15] also reported significant disruption in corn stover by applying freezing–thawing technique coupled with ammonia pretreatment to achieve enhanced biomethane production. Moreover, alkaline pretreatments coupled with ultrasonic application also have positive effects on sludge disintegration, as well as methane production [16,17]. Wang et al. [18] suggested remarkable decline in lignin percent by combining ultrasonic pretreatment with lower concentrations of NaOH (2%), which also helped in daily biogas enhancement compared to untreated or only NaOH pretreated rice straw. Phuttaro et al. [19] achieved 40% gain in methane production when optimal size and sonication dosage were applied in combination.

The ultrasonic application is beneficial to reduce the process time and solvent requirements with effective disrupting potentials. Ultrasonication potentially promotes some chemical processes like hydrolysis, esterification, and transesterification of carbohydrates in lignocellulosic biomasses [20]. The aqueous phase ultrasonic application results in generation of repeated cavitation bubbles, which further enlarge to unstable stage and finally burst into high energy units [21]. These high energy units with localized high temperatures can cause effective disruption to the outer surface of the lignocellulosic biomass. Ultrasonic applications have been widely applied in different environmental processes, such as enzyme and intracellular recovery, pollutant remediation and anaerobic digestion of agricultural waste as well as wastewater sludge [22]. However, single ultrasonic pretreatment may have limited benefits because the physicochemical characteristics of the lignocellulosic biomass require an additional affinity solvent to release the organic content by dissolution in aqueous phase pretreatments. Elalami et al. [23] suggested positive effect of mild ultrasonic combination with alkali pretreatment, to increase fiber degradation and methane production compared to single ultrasonic or alkali pretreatment of olive pomase. Moreover, the ultrasonication parameters like temperature, intensity and time duration are the most important and may affect the performance of ultrasonication during pretreatment. Most studies suggest mild ultrasonic intensity (~ 20 kHz) and ambient temperature conditions, for better delignification of the wheat straw [24].

Therefore, this study focused on the combination of ultrasonic application using different KOH concentrations on WS to determine its effects on physiochemical properties of WS. Furthermore, the significance of pretreatment was evaluated by investigating the digester performance and increased biogasification. The application of bath ultrasonication during pretreatment was hypothesized to handle bulk quantity of WS in less time. This technique can be helpful in industrial pretreatments, where limited storage for biomass is preferred.


2 Materials and Methods


2.1 Raw materials

The air-dried WS was collected from an agricultural field located at Changping district, Beijing. Initially, WS was chopped into smaller size of 5–10 cm, using a paper chopper and was further crushed to a mesh size of 5-10 mm, using hammer mill (Fe130, Staida Co., Tianjin). The inoculum (activated seed sludge) was obtained from an anaerobic digester operating at mesophilic temperature and treating dairy manure (Nanwu biogas plant in Shunyi District, Beijing, China). The initial characteristics of WS and inoculum are shown in Table 1 .


2.2 Ultrasonic assisted KOH pretreatment (KOH Upt )

The ultrasonic assisted potassium hydroxide pretreatment (KOH_Upt) was applied in 1 L of bottles. Fig. 1 shows the experimental setup and ultrasonic conditions applied during pretreatment. Three concentrations of KOH, 2%, 4%, and 6% (w/w) were applied. pH was used to predict the optimum time required for pretreatment process [25]. A ratio of 1:0.02–0.06:6 (w/w) for WS:KOH:H_2O was used during pretreatment. A required weight of KOH was first added in each bottle containing a pre-calculated amount of distilled water. In order to avoid any exothermic heat effects, KOH solutions were left for a short time interval (3–5 min) to achieve equilibrium. Finally, WS was added to KOH solution and was thoroughly mixed to attain the uniform distribution of moisture content, before starting ultrasonic process. The pretreatment was carried out in an ultrasonic bath (KH5200DE, Nanjing Xiao Equipment Company, China). The reason to apply bath ultrasonication process was to effectively handle the bulk amount of biomass in single run for each KOH concentration. Therefore, 50 g TSL^−1 of WS was applied in triplicate during each pretreatment. The ultrasonic bath was operated at temperature of (22 ± 1) °C (ambient temperature) and at constant intensity of 20 kHz. The water level in the ultrasonication bath was maintained above the biomass in order to provide maximum ultrasonication effect throughout the bottles. During ultrasonication, the bath temperature gradually increased, however, it was further calibrated to set point value by controlling bath water temperature. pH was recorded after every 4 h and the pretreatment was stopped when pH reached to a steady value. Finally, the pretreated materials were stored at 4 °C until the next use. A portion of pretreated sample was collected and stored separately as solid and liquid fractions for analysis.


2.3 Anaerobic digestion

Batch anaerobic digestion test was applied to untreated and KOH_Upt WS, to evaluate the effect of pretreatment on digester performance. A 50 g TS·L^−1 of WS was added in 1 L of digester bottles with 0.8 L of working volume. A 0.3 inoculum to substrate ratio (I/S) was used in all digesters, which was taken according to the suggestion from Zhang and Zhang [26]. All the tests in this study were prepared in triplicates. Two separate bottles with only inoculum (15 g TS⋅L^−1) were also placed in the bath as control. The remaining working volume was filled by adding distilled water. Nitrogen gas was purged to confirm removal of any residual oxygen in bottles. Finally, all the bottles were tightly sealed to maintain anaerobic conditions. The operating temperature was maintained at (35 ± 1) °C throughout the digestion period. All bottles were manually shaken for 2 min. twice a day, for appropriate mixing throughout the digestion [27].


2.4 Analytical methods

The total solid (TS), volatile solid (VS), total alkalinity (TA), and total ammonia nitrogen (TAN) were determined as per APHA standards [28]. The Lignin Hemicellulose and cellulose (LHC) content were analyzed by a fiber analyzer (ANKOM A2000i, Macedon, NY 14502), according to the procedure suggested by VanSoest et al. [29]. The daily biogas production from each digester was measured using water displacement method. This total daily volume was then converted to a standard temperature and pressure (STP) volume, using the ideal gas law. In order to evaluate the gas composition, the samples were collected from the connection pipes from all the bottles (on daily basis) and the respective methane contents were analyzed by a gas chromatograph (SP-2100, HJAT, China), equipped with a detector molecular sieve packed with stainless steel column (TDX-01) with length of 2.0 mm and 3.0 mm diameter, and connected to thermal conductivity detector (TCD), using argon as carrier gas. The temperatures for injector, column and detector were kept at 140 °C, 150 °C, and 150 °C, respectively. Similarly, the liquid KOH_Upt samples were first centrifuged to obtain liquid supernatants, which were then filtered through 0.22 μm membrane and the total volatile fatty acid (TVFA), such as ethanol, acetic acid, propionic acid, valeric acid, and butyric acid were determined by a gas chromatograph (SHIMADZU, GC2014) equipped with a flame ionization detector (FID) and a 30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm column (Agilent, DB-WAX). The physical morphology of the untreated and KOH_Upt samples were analyzed by using the scanning electron microscope (SEM) analyzer with high resolution (Hitachi S-4700 model). The Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were obtained by using FTIR spectrophotometer (Nicolet 5DXC) on a KBr disk containing 1% of ground samples. All the values were mean ± standard deviation (SD) (n = 3).

3 Results


3.1 pH variation during pretreatment

The addition of the ultrasonic medium during KOH pretreatment showed significant influence on the physiochemical characteristics of WS. Fig. 2 , shows the variation in pH values during KOH_Upt. A large drop in pH was observed at the initial stage, which gradually decreased after 24 h of pretreatment and reached to negligible change after 36 h, irrespective of KOH concentration. Therefore, 36 h was selected as the optimum time for all KOH_Upt. Yang et al. reported 7 days of pretreatment time for effective disruption in WS, when treated with 4% urea and 80% moisture content at 35 °C of pretreatment temperature [33]. The reduction in pretreatment time in this study was attributed to the fact that combining ultrasonic medium during KOH pretreatment was more effective for further breakdown of linkage between hemicellulose and cellulose contents of WS, which allowed increased hydrolysis rate during pretreatment. This could be explained as the mechanical disruption by ultrasonic cavitations helped in destroying the external structure of WS and provided more access to the hydroxyl ion (OH^−) to extract more organic content. Therefore, the KOH_Upt samples showed an accelerated pH drop with enhanced saccharification.


3.2 Effect of KOH Upt  on TVFA production

In order to determine the significance of KOH_Upt on WS, the untreated samples were also prepared by adding a similar amount of distilled water and placed in parallel as control, without KOH and/or ultrasonic application. Fig. 3 shows the composition of different volatile fatty acid (VFA) productions from untreated and KOH_Upt samples. The results showed a significant increment in TVFA productions with increasing KOH concentration. The 6% KOH_Upt achieved highest TVFA productions (3189 ± 10) mg·L^−1, compared to the untreated WS samples (451 ± 8) mg·L^−1. This was 7.07 folds more TVFA production compared to untreated sample. The methane production and the digester efficiency largely depend upon the amount of TVFA (in particular acetate) present in the anaerobic digester [34]. Acetic acid was the major content in all TVFA composition of KOH_Upt samples. The values for acetic acid concentrations in 2%, 4%, and 6% KOH_Upt samples were (1723 ± 17) mg·L^−1, (2792 ± 16) mg·L^−1, and (2974 ± 21) mg·L^−1, respectively. These values were 5.49, 8.90, and 9.48 folds more than the untreated samples, respectively. The untreated WS samples accounted only (313.57 ± 4.01) mg·L^−1 of the acetate concentration. These values confirmed the effective hydrolysis of WS, during KOH_Upt. Similarly, the concentrations of other TVFA components, like butyrate, valerate, and propionate, were also higher as compared to the untreated samples. However, their respective proportion remained significantly less compared to acetate values.


3.3 Effect of KOH Upt  on physiochemical structure of wheat straw

The SEM images of untreated and KOH_Upt samples were captured to compare the physical changes in the structure of WS (Fig. 4 ). The untreated WS samples (Fig. 4a) showed a rigid smooth surface with uniformly attached long fibers, indicating less surface accessibility for anaerobic microbes, whereas the surface structure of the 2%, 4%, and 6% KOH_Upt samples (Fig. 4 b, c, and d, respectively) was significantly destroyed after pretreatment. The cracks were evident and fiber detachments were visible on the outer surface. The appearance of numerous small holes with increased pore size also confirmed the potential disruption of the lignin layer by ultrasonic application. These breakdowns in the pretreated samples were due to: (i) loosing of fiber attachment, which were caused by swelling effect of hydroxyl ion, and (ii) simultaneous breaking of intra polymer attachments between the outer lignin layer (by ultrasonic medium), allowing in further penetration of OH^− ions to release more organic contents from WS [35].

The FT-IR analysis of the samples also confirmed the changes in chemical structure of WS (Fig. 5 ). The variation in peak intensities and shifting was observed at different wavenumbers. Obvious changes appeared in the region from 1800 to 500 cm^−1 which is considered as finger print region. The peak 1735 cm^−1 represents the carboxyl attachments between hemicellulose and lignin [36]. These peaks appeared reducing with increasing KOH concentration in the pretreated samples. Similar reductions were reported by Momayez et al. [37] when liquid fraction of biogas digestate was used in thermal pretreatment of WS. Similarly, the band at 1600 cm^−1 is assigned to the bending mode of water, as hemicelluloses have strong affinity with water [25]. A band shift was observed from 1627 cm^−1 to 1600 cm^−1 in pretreated samples indicating successful disruption in hemicellulose during pretreatment. The bands at 1535 cm^−1 represents aromatic skeletal vibrations of lignin content and the band at 1245 cm^−1 corresponds to CO stretching [38]. These peaks were observed diminishing gradually in the pretreated samples. Moreover, the peak at 1370 cm^−1 reflects CH bending also shifted to 1382 cm^−1 in pretreated samples, due to partial hydrolysis of lignin and hemicellulose contents [36]. These variations in FTIR spectrums confirmed the potential chemical disruption in the WS structure after KOH_Upt.


3.4 Biogasification and methane production

The daily and cumulative biogas and biomethane yields are shown in Fig. 6 . Several peaks were observed at different time spans of AD, for all digesters. Results showed that the KOH_Upt digesters produced higher values of biogas and biomethane productions compared to untreated digesters, at any stage of the digestion process (Fig. 6a). 4% KOH_Upt achieved highest cumulative biogas yield (CBY) of (555.49 ± 6.96) ml·g^−1VS_loaded in 45 d of digestion time (Fig. 6b). Similarly, 2% and 6% KOH_Upt digesters produced (445.93 ± 10.97) and (462.09 ± 7.03) ml·g^−1 of biogas, respectively, whereas, CBY from the untreated WS digesters amounted (411.71 ± 11.07) ml·g^−1 only. The biogasification from 2%, 4%, and 6% KOH_Upt digesters was 8.31%, 34.92% and 12.24% higher than the untreated digesters respectively. This enhancement is attributed to the presence of more hydrolyzed organic matter in KOH_Upt digesters.

On the other hand, the methane peaks of all the KOH_Upt digester appeared at higher levels, compared to untreated digesters (Fig. 6c). The highest peak of specific methane yield (SMY) from 4%KOH_Upt digester reached to (21.25 ± 0.36) ml·(g VS_loaded)^−1·d^−1, on 13 d of digestion process whereas, the untreated WS digesters showed highest peak on 18 d, with (12.47 ± 0.28) ml·(g VS_loaded)^−1·d^−1 of SMY. These results reflected increased methanogenic activity in KOH_Upt digesters due to presence of more TVFA compounds. However, the untreated WS digesters had less methane production due to intact bonding between the lignocellulosic contents resulting in less permeability of anaerobic microbe to the organic content in WS. The cumulative methane yield (CMY) from untreated, 2%, 4% and 6% KOH_Upt digestion reached at (192 ± 7.01), (221 ± 6.85), (282 ± 6.90), and (229 ± 7.68) ml·(g VS_loaded)^−1, respectively (Fig. 6d) much higher compared to untreated digesters. Jaffar et al. reported 258 ml·(g VS_loaded)^−1 of CMY during 50 d of digestion time, when 6% KOH pretreatments were applied to WS [8]. Similarly, Liu et al. achieved 317 ml·(g VS_loaded)^−1 of CMY in 25 d of thermophilic AD process, by treating WS with 50% KOH concentrations [39]. In this study, the increased CMY from 4% KOH_Upt digesters was comparable with the findings in literature, when considering lower digestion temperature and low KOH concentrations.


3.5 Biodegradability

The biodegradability (B _d) of untreated and KOH_Upt digesters was calculated using Eq. (3). B _d for untreated, 2%, 4%, and 6% KOH_Upt digesters was 46.58%, 53.68%, 68.50%, and 55.67% respectively. These values of B _d can justify the higher biogasification potential by KOH_Upt digesters. The untreated digesters were found to be less biodegradable as compared to KOH_Upt digesters. Similarly, the effectiveness of biodegradability can be evaluated by calculating the technical digestion time (T _80), the time taken by digester to produce 80% of cumulative biomethane [40]. Reduced T _80 values of 2% and 4% KOH_Upt digesters suggested faster biodegradation during AD. T _80 of 4% KOH_Upt digesters were achieved on the 19 d of AD process, while the corresponding T _80 values for 2% and 6% KOH_Upt digesters appeared on 18 d and 23 d, respectively. Furthermore, it was observed that the 4% KOH_Upt digester produced 231.39 ml·(g VS_loaded)^−1 of biomethane in 19 days of AD process, which was even higher than the CMY of untreated digesters (191.76 ml·g^−1 VS_loaded). The respective T _80 of untreated digesters were achieved on 21st day of AD, with 155.07 ml·g^−1VS_loaded of biomethane yield. These results suggested that addition of ultrasonic medium during KOH pretreatment had significant potential in increasing the biodegradation rate during AD along with enhanced biomethane production.


3.6 Modified Gompertz model

Modified Gompertz model (MGM), Eq. (4) was used to compare the predicted and experimental methane productions. Table 2 shows estimated parameters of the model for untreated and KOH_Upt digesters. R ^2 ranging between 0.993–0.998 suggested successful simulation of the model, for this study. The lower λ and higher μ _m reflects faster startup and better biodegradation, respectively [41]. The λ values for 2% and 4% KOH_Upt digesters were significantly less compared to untreated and 6%KOH_Upt digesters. Similarly, higher μ _m values from KOH_Upt digester were in agreement to the daily methane production trends. However, the experimental maximum methane yield rate from 4% KOH_Upt digesters (21.25 ± 0.37) ml·g^−1⋅d^−1 were much higher than the model predictions. The B _o values from model predictions were also in agreement with the EMY values. These results indicated that application of KOH_Upt on WS enhanced biodegradability rates with increased methane production. The data was statistically analyzed using one-way ANOVA (Origin Pro, Version 9.0). All the values were found significantly different at α = 0.05.


3.7 Digestate stability

Apart from effective degradation of the biomass, it is also necessary to focus on the post characteristics of the digestate. Stable ranges of various parameters may be helpful in utilizing the anaerobic digestate as alternative bio-fertilizers in agriculture [42]. Table 3 shows the various parameters of digestate after AD. The final pH values for all digesters ranged between 7.4 and 7.7, which were within the stable range of digester effluents [43]. Similarly, the total ammonia nitrogen (TAN) ranged between 467 and 639 mg·L^−1, which is considered in the lower range for active methanogenic activity during AD [44]. Insufficient total alkalinity (TA) refers to unstable denitrification in effluent treatments [45]. The TA value for all the digesters ranged in between 4.13 and 6.34 g CaCO_3⋅L^−1 and were also above the buffering limit for nitrification effect [46]. Moreover, the TVFA results from all KOH_Upt digester effluents were significantly reduced, showing maximum consumption of TVFA content during AD. Finally, the TVFAs/TA ratio ranging between 0.05 and 0.06 was much less than the threshold limits for stable digester performance [47]. These results confirmed the effective bioconversion of WS by KOH_Upt. Therefore, 4% KOH_Upt of WS can be proposed to be suitable for higher biogasification and effective system stability.


4 Conclusions

Ultrasonic assisted KOH pretreatment was applied on WS with different KOH concentrations. Pretreatment time was successfully reduced to 36 h for all KOH concentrations tested. Significant increase in TVFA production along with availability of more acetic acid in VFA content reflected the release of more organic content in KOH_Upt samples. Moreover, SEM analysis and FTIR results revealed successful disruption in outer structure of WS by ultrasonic combination during KOH pretreatment. The enhanced biogasification and biomethane production (555 and 282 ml·(g VS_loaded)^−1, from 4% KOH_Upt confirmed the potential improvement in pretreatment efficiency. The highest biodegradability of 68.5% from 4% KOH_Upt AD also indicated enhanced bioconversion of the biomass. Finally, lower value in of TVFA/TA, TAN and other parameter of KOH_Upt digestate indicated more stability in performance during AD. Therefore, this study recommends combined ultrasonic application during KOH pretreatment, for effective hydrolysis of WS while using 4% KOH concentrations for enhanced biogasification and digester performance. Moreover, the potential to handle bulk amount of WS using this pretreatment can be helpful in industrial applications.
