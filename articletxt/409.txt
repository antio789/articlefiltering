TITLE: Continuous anaerobic digestion of zucchini stem after pretreatment


1 Introduction

The world’s demand for energy resources is rapidly increasing with the growth of the economy and population. Fossil fuels are the most commonly used energy resource, accounting for 80% of current energy needs, while excessive use tends to cause energy scarcity and serious environmental problems (Gao et al., 2019; Luo and Liu, 2019). Therefore, many countries are devoted to developing renewable energy to reduce the consumption of fossil fuels (Gao et al., 2019; Luo and Liu, 2019; Qiao et al., 2019). Biomethane is a promising renewable energy generated by anaerobic digestion (AD) (Hu et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2020), which is a widely applied technology for converting biomass wastes into clean energy (Fu et al., 2020). In recent years, an increasing number of large-scale biogas plants have been established in China, while many biogas plants lack a sustainable and abundant feedstock supply to satisfy the continuous operation needs of AD projects (Khalid et al., 2019).

Zucchini stem (ZS) is the main by-product of zucchini, which is a popular vegetable in China with an annual production of 8.0 million tons (TRIDGE, 2019). Every year, almost 2.4 million tons of ZS, which has high moisture and abundant organic matter, are discarded in the process of growth and machining, leading to resource wastes and environmental pollution (Ravi et al., 2018; Salehiyoun et al., 2019). Currently, ZS is generally made into animal fodder and organic fertilizer, and even an be used as precursor of conductive carbon-based nanomaterial due to the electromagnetic property (Hammi et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2020a,b; Xie et al., 2020). However, the utilization efficiencies of those applications are so limited; and thus, AD might be an alternative application, which could simultaneously convert ZS into high-value methane resource as well as relieve environmental pressure associated with waste disposal (Bi et al., 2020; Ruan et al., 2019). Besides, zucchini is a typical seasonal crop mainly cultivated in greenhouses in China throughout the year, which makes ZS a potential and sustainable substrate for AD plant (Gu et al., 2020). Nevertheless, the recalcitrant lignocellulosic structure of ZS restricts the hydrolysis rate during AD and causes low methane production, which makes effective pretreatment necessary before AD (Tisma et al., 2018). Pretreatments could potentially enhance the activity of hydrolytic bacteria and hydrolysis rate during AD, which may affect the subsequent reactions and microbial communities, further influencing the efficiency and stability of AD system (Abraham et al., 2020). Our previous studies showed that 4% NaOH, 2% KOH, and 4% alkaline hydrogen peroxide (AHP) pretreatments (mass concentration) could effectively break the lignocellulosic structure, reduce the contents of lignin, and significantly promote the methane yields of ZS in batch AD (Gu et al., 2020).

Compared to batch mode, continuous AD is more useful for industrial applications (Ruffino et al., 2015). The organic loading rate (OLR) is one of the most crucial parameters in continuous AD systems (Zahan et al., 2018). Generally, a high OLR is preferred for disposal efficiency and economic benefits. However, the too high OLR could lead to the rapid increase of volatile fatty acids (VFAs) and the pH decline (Khan et al., 2016), causing instability and inhibition of the AD system (Zhao et al., 2019). Total alkalinity (TA) offers buffer capacity to resist fast acidification shock by VFAs accumulation (Ma et al., 2020). Generally, the VFAs to TA ratio (VFAs/TA) is regarded as an indicator to evaluate stability of AD system and the instable threshold of VFAs/TA is 0.40 (de la Lama et al., 2017; Ma et al., 2020). Besides, total ammonia nitrogen (TAN) is another crucial indicator that affects the system stability, which is supposed to be less than 1500 mg L^−1 (Liu et al., 2018). The above-mentioned parameters, including pH, VFAs, TA, and TAN, are important to assess the stability of AD system (Li et al., 2018a). While to date, no literature about the methane conversion efficiency and system stability of continuous AD of raw and pretreated ZS has been reported.

This study aims to investigate the methane production performance of raw and pretreated ZS during continuous AD at different OLRs and to evaluate the system stability to maximize methane conversion efficiency from ZS.


2 Materials and methods


2.1 Substrate and inoculum

ZS was collected from a farm in Weifang, Shandong province, China, and was cut into 3–5 mm pieces before used. The anaerobic sludge obtained from Donghuashan Biogas Plant was used as inoculum. The sludge was kept at room temperature for two weeks to remove the background of methane production and then the supernate of sludge was removed.


2.2 Pretreatment methods

In this study, 2% KOH, 4% NaOH, and 4% AHP pretreatments (mass concentration) were performed on ZS before continuous AD experiment. The pH value of AHP solution was adjusted to 11.5 by adding NaOH. The moisture content of all the pretreatment systems was set to 95%, and the weight of ZS and solution for each condition was calculated by Eq. (1) (Li et al., 2015). Then, the ZS and solution were mixed uniformly and stored at room temperature for 24 h. After pretreatment, ZS was separated from mixtures and used for continuous AD. 2.3 Continuous anaerobic digestion

The continuous AD experiment was performed in a 1000 mL bottle with a 500 mL working volume that was placed in a shaker with a frequency of 120 rpm at 37 °C. ZS was first fed into the digester according to the initial setting OLR (1.0 gVS L^−1 d^−1); and then, sufficient inoculum (about 250 mL) and tap water were supplied until getting working volume. After the methane yields (daily methane yield, DMY; and volumetric methane yield, VMY) becoming stable, OLR increased with a gradient of 0.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, until instability of the AD system occurred. At OLRs of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, and 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the hydraulic retention times (HRTs) were 50.0, 33.3, 25.0, 20.0, 16.7, and 14.3 d, respectively. Before feeding substrate into the reactor, the effluent was discharged and collected at a fixed time every day. And then, nitrogen was injected into the bottle to replace the atmosphere and maintain anaerobic conditions. Each condition was conducted in duplicate, and two blanks of inoculum without ZS were used for correction.


2.4 Analytical methods

The total solids (TS) and volatile solids (VS) of the ZS, inoculum, and effluent were detected by APHA standard methods (APHA, 2005). The contents of C, H, and N were measured by an elemental analyzer, and the O content was estimated by the equation C + H + O + N = 99 . 5 % on a VS basis (Ning et al., 2016). The lignocellulosic contents, including cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, were measured by the Van Soest method with an ANKOM 2000 fiber analyzer (Shen et al., 2019).

DMY and VMY were determined based on the biogas yield, which was calculated by Eq. (2) (Li et al., 2018a). The headspace pressure of reactor was measured by a 3151 WAL-BMP-Test system pressure gauge (WAL Messund Regelsysteme GmbH, Oldenburg, Lower Saxony, Germany) (Gu et al., 2020). A gas chromatograph (GC) equipped with thermal conductivity detector and Agilent Hayesep Q column (Agilent 7890B, Santa Clara, CA, USA) was used to detect the methane content (MC).

The pH, TAN, TA, and VFAs of the effluent were analyzed every four days. An LE438 pH electrode (Mettler Toledo, USA) was utilized to detect the pH value, and the TAN was measured by Nessler’s reagent spectrophotometry (Xue et al., 2019). TA was detected by the titrimetric method with sulfuric acid using Hach reagent (Li et al., 2018a). In addition, VFAs were measured by an Agilent GC (7890 A, Agilent, USA) equipped with DB-wax capillary column (30 × 0.53 × 1.0 μm) and flame ionization detector (Shen et al., 2019). Moreover, scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Hitachi S-4700, Japan) was applied to observe the structural variation in ZS after pretreatment.

3 Results and discussion


3.1 Characteristics of ZS and inoculum

The characteristics of the ZS and inoculum are given in Table 1. The TS and VS/TS of ZS were 8.37% and 78.11%, respectively, indicating the high moisture and organic contents of ZS, which are desirable for AD (Zhao et al., 2017). The contents of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, which constitute the complex lignocellulosic structure, were determined to be 36.93%, 10.25%, and 11.51%, respectively. Moreover, according to the elemental composition, the MMY of ZS was 419.0 mL gVS − 1, as calculated by Eq. (3) and Eq. (4).




3.2 SEM analysis of raw and pretreated ZS

SEM images of raw and pretreated ZS are presented in Fig. 1. As shown in Fig. 1a, raw ZS showed a smooth and compact surface. After KOH, NaOH, and AHP pretreatment (Fig. 1b, Fig. 1c, and Fig. 1d, respectively), the surface of ZS was seriously destroyed with many cracks and holes, illustrating that the porosities of pretreated ZS were increased (Abraham et al., 2020). These variations might enhance the attachment of anaerobes which would influence the conversion efficiency during AD (Ramm et al., 2019).




3.3 Methane production performance of pretreated ZS


3.3.1 Methane content

The MC of pretreated ZS at different OLRs is shown in Fig. 2a. For KOH-pretreated and raw ZS, the MC remained relatively stable and high (64.7%–70.1% and 59.9%–65.7%, respectively) at OLRs of 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, indicating that methanogens might be relatively active (Alvarez and Lidén, 2008). Nevertheless, the MC of NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS was only stable at OLRs of 1.0–1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1 and showed an obvious decline when the OLR increased to 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, implying that the activity of methanogens could be inhibited at 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 (Alvarez and Lidén, 2008).




3.3.2 Daily methane yield

DMY is an index used to assess the conversion efficiency of the substrate during the AD process (Wang et al., 2014). The DMY of pretreated ZS at different OLRs is shown in Fig. 2b. At OLRs of 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the DMY of raw and KOH-pretreated ZS was stable, while the mean DMY presented a slightly diminished trend corresponding to the increasing OLR, which was in accordance with the results of Mu et al. (2018) who digested organic fraction of municipal solid waste by applying semi-continuous AD, also performing a gradually declined DMY (from 430 to 344 mL gVS − 1 d^−1) with the increased OLR. When the OLR rose up to 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, a significant decline in DMY was observed in both raw and KOH-pretreated ZS, which might be due to the accumulation of VFAs at high OLRs in the AD system (de la Lama et al., 2017). The mean DMY of KOH-pretreated ZS (236.0–311.7 mL g VS − 1 d^−1) was 5.6%–34.9% higher than that of raw ZS (182.1–280.1 mL g VS − 1 d^−1) at 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, especially at OLRs of 2.5 and 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 (34.9% and 29.6%, respectively) (p < 0.05). But for NaOH and AHP pretreatment, DMY was only stable at 1.0–1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1 (298.7–371.0 mL g VS − 1 d^−1 and 347.8–353.1 mL g VS − 1 d^−1, respectively) and rapidly declined at 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1. The maximum OLR with stable DMY of NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS (1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1) was much lower than that of KOH-pretreated ZS (3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1).


3.3.3 Volumetric methane yield

VMY is an indicator that represents the methane production performance of a reactor based on the reactor volume (Ma et al., 2020). It is a crucial parameter for reactor engineering design and economic estimation (Ma et al., 2020). Fig. 2c shows the VMY of ZS during continuous AD at different OLRs. As the OLR increased from 1.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 to 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the VMY of raw ZS increased from 0.282 L L^−1 d^−1 to 0.546 L L^−1 d^−1, and the VMY of KOH-pretreated ZS increased from 0.312 L L^−1 d^−1 to 0.708 L L^−1 d^−1. When the OLR increased to 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the VMY of both raw and KOH-pretreated ZS showed a continuous decline. In the research of Alvarez and Lidén (2008) who co-digested solid slaughterhouse waste, manure, and fruit and vegetable waste in semi-continuous AD, VMY also continuously increased at relatively lower OLR, while quickly declined at high OLR of 3.8 gVS L^−1 d^−1, highly consistent with our results. Both raw ZS and KOH-pretreated ZS achieved a high VMY at an OLR of 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, and the maximum VMY of KOH-pretreated ZS (0.708 L L^−1 d^−1) was 29.7% higher than that of raw ZS (0.546 L L^−1 d^−1) (p < 0.05). For NaOH and AHP pretreatments, the VMY increased with the OLR promoted from 1.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 to 1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1 and then rapidly declined at OLR of 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1. Both NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS produced the highest VMY at 1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1 (0.450 and 0.523 L L^−1 d^−1, respectively), which was significantly lower than the maximum VMY of KOH-pretreated ZS (0.708 L L^−1 d^−1) (p < 0.01).


3.3.4 Hydraulic retention time

HRT is another important parameter affecting AD performance (Bi et al., 2020). Shortening HRT can not only lower the costs of reactor construction and operation but also increase the substrate disposal amount (Wandera et al., 2019). In this study, at OLRs of 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, and 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the HRTs were 50.0, 33.3, 25.0, 20.0, 16.7, and 14.3 d, respectively. The methane production performance of raw and KOH-pretreated ZS was stable at 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 with a short HRT (16.7 d), indicating relatively high disposal and reactor efficiency (Khan et al., 2016). NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS only performed steadily at 1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, corresponding to a relatively long HRT (33.3 d).

Overall, compared with NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS, KOH-pretreated ZS could not only perform stably at a relatively high OLR of 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 with a short HRT of 16.7 d but also significantly enhance the VMY of ZS, indicating high efficiency of ZS disposal and high methane production performance (Wang et al., 2014). Thus, KOH pretreatment was regarded as the most effective pretreatment for continuous AD of ZS.


3.4 Biodegradability and VS removal

The B d and VS removal of different pretreated ZS samples are shown in Fig. 3. At 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the B d values of raw and KOH-pretreated ZS were relatively high at 43.5%–66.8% and 56.3%–74.4%, respectively. KOH-pretreated ZS exhibited a higher B d than raw ZS at each OLR, especially at 2.5 and 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, suggesting that the biodegradability of ZS was improved after KOH pretreatment (Gu et al., 2020). Nevertheless, when the OLR increased to 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the B d values of NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS rapidly declined to 20.3% and 17.7%, respectively, which were much lower than that of raw ZS (p < 0.05). In addition, the VS removal of both raw and KOH-pretreated ZS was higher than 65% at 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, while NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS only achieved relatively high VS removal (76.4%–78.6% and 74.6%–81.3%, respectively) at OLRs of 1.0–1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1.




3.5 AD system stability of different pretreated ZS samples

The stability parameters for AD systems with different pretreated ZS types are shown in Fig. 4. For raw ZS (Fig. 4a), the pH (7.3–7.5) was in the proper range of AD operation (6.8–7.5), and TAN (660.4–917.8 mg L^−1) was lower than the inhibition concentration (1500 mg L^−1) at OLR of 1.0–3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1 (Liu et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2019). TA increased gradually from 3933 mg L^−1 to 6393 mg L^−1 as the OLR increased from 1.0 to 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1. At 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the VFAs concentration was in a low and stable range (0–121.8 mg L^−1) that was much lower than the inhibitory concentration (2000–6000 mg L^−1) (Mu et al., 2018), but VFAs showed an increasing tendency at 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1. VFAs/TA is another essential indicator for estimating the stability of AD systems, and a VFAs/TA under 0.40 is preferred (Ma et al., 2020). As seen in Fig. 4a, VFAs/TA was in the range of 0–0.02 at 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 and then increased to 0.18 at 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1. In general, the AD system with raw ZS was stable at 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 and existed a possibility of acidification at 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1.

As shown in Fig. 4a, the pH of the AD system after KOH pretreatment (7.4–7.6) at 1.0–3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1 was slightly higher than the proper range. TAN decreased from 835.7 mg L^−1 to 172.7 mg L^−1 as OLR increased from 1.0 to 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1. TA increased from 4219 mg L^−1 to 7940 mg L^−1 as the OLR increased from 1.0 to 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, which was 8.1%–71.1% higher than that of raw ZS, indicating that the buffer capacity of the AD system was improved after KOH pretreatment (Ruan et al., 2019). At 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, VFAs and VFAs/TA were both low (0–208.6 mg L^−1 and 0–0.04, respectively). When the OLR increased to 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, VFAs continuously increased to 2477.2 mg L^−1, which reached the inhibitory concentration range of 2000–6000 mg L^−1 (Mu et al., 2018), suggesting VFAs accumulation in the AD system. VFAs/TA also rapidly increased to 0.37, which was close to the threshold of 0.40. These results indicated that the AD system with KOH-pretreated ZS performed well at 1.0–3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 and that VFAs started to accumulate at 3.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, which was similar to the results for raw ZS.

As shown in Fig. 4b, the pH values of the AD systems with NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS (7.6–7.9 and 7.5–7.8, respectively) were higher than those of raw ZS. Moreover, the TA values after NaOH and AHP pretreatment (4646–11 457 mg L^−1 and 4545–12 763 mg L^−1, respectively) were much higher than that of raw ZS (3933–6393 mg L^−1). This high pH and TA might be caused by alkali residue in the AD system after NaOH and AHP pretreatment. At 1.0 and 1.5 g VS L^−1 d^−1, VFAs and VFAs/TA were relatively low. However, when the OLR reached 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, the VFAs concentrations of NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS increased sharply to 12200.8 mg L^−1 and 11389.6 mg L^−1, respectively, which far exceeded the inhibitory concentration (2000–6000 mg L^−1). The VFAs/TA of NaOH- and AHP-pretreated ZS reached 1.10 and 1.07, respectively, indicating that the buffering capacity of AD systems could not withstand the severely rapid VFAs accumulation which eventually resulted in the limited methane yields (Ma et al., 2020). Overall, the extreme accumulation of VFAs destroyed the AD system stability of ZS after NaOH and AHP pretreatment at an OLR of 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, which explained the poor methane production performance at higher OLRs.




4 Conclusion

This study investigated the performance of continuous AD systems for the treatment of raw ZS, KOH-pretreated ZS, NaOH-pretreated ZS, and AHP-pretreated ZS at different OLRs, respectively. The results showed that 2% KOH-pretreatment was the optimal condition for continuous AD of ZS, achieving not only stable performance at a relatively high OLR of 3.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1 but also the highest VMY of 0.708 L L^−1 d^−1, which was 29.7% higher than that of raw ZS. Nevertheless, due to the rapid accumulation of VFAs, the AD systems with 4% NaOH- and 4% AHP-pretreated ZS became unstable when the OLR increased to 2.0 g VS L^−1 d^−1, indicating that these pretreatments may not be suitable for single-phase continuous AD. To further improve methane production, more research on two-phase digestion is necessary in the future. The findings of this study provide a reference for the stable and efficient operation of continuous AD for pretreated ZS, which may facilitate the future practical application of ZS waste.
